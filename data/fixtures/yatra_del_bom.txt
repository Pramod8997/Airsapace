**About Delhi**

Delhi, India's capital, blends Mughal-era grandeur with the everyday energy of one of the world's largest metro regions. Travellers booking Delhi to Mumbai flights on Yatra.com often extend their trip by a day or two to explore the city's heritage forts, bustling bazaars, and street-food lanes that stretch from the narrow alleys of Old Delhi to the planned avenues of New Delhi. Politics, history, cuisine, and commerce all intersect here, making Delhi one of India's most-visited gateway cities for both domestic and international travellers. With over 165+ daily flights connecting Delhi to Mumbai alone, the capital remains one of the most accessible cities in India to fly into and explore at your own pace.

### **What are the best places to visit in Delhi for monuments and heritage?**

Delhi holds three UNESCO World Heritage Sites within its city limits. The Red Fort, built by Mughal emperor Shah Jahan, remains the most recognisable symbol of the city and the venue for India's Independence Day address each year. Jama Masjid, one of India's largest mosques, sits a short walk away in the heart of Old Delhi. Qutub Minar, a 73-metre sandstone tower dating to the 12th century, is known for its intricate carvings and inscriptions. Humayun's Tomb, an early example of Mughal garden-tomb architecture, is widely considered the design precursor to the Taj Mahal. India Gate, a war memorial honouring soldiers of the British Indian Army, remains a popular evening gathering spot for locals and visitors alike. Most of these landmarks sit within a 10–15 km radius of Connaught Place, making a single-day Delhi heritage circuit easy to plan around a short layover or weekend trip.

### **Where can you experience the best Delhi street food and shopping markets?**

Delhi's food culture is one of its biggest draws for travellers flying in from across India and abroad. Chandni Chowk is the city's oldest and most famous food street, known for chaat, parathas, jalebi, and kebabs served from decades-old stalls that have operated for generations. Shoppers head to Sarojini Nagar and Janpath for budget fashion, Dilli Haat for handicrafts and regional cuisine representing states from across India, and Khan Market or Connaught Place for a more contemporary mix of cafés, boutiques, and dining. Each Delhi market reflects a different facet of the city — from centuries-old trade lanes to air-conditioned retail hubs — giving first-time visitors a sense of how the capital has layered old and new over the decades.

### **When is the Delhi Republic Day Parade held and how does it affect travel plans?**

Every January 26, Delhi hosts the Republic Day Parade along Kartavya Path, showcasing military formations, state tableaux representing India's cultural diversity, and an Indian Air Force fly-past. Travellers flying into Delhi around this period can also see government buildings illuminated at night and cultural programs held across the city, making it one of the busiest travel weeks for flights to the capital. Hotel and flight demand for Delhi typically spikes in the days surrounding January 26, so travellers planning a Delhi to Mumbai or [Mumbai to Delhi trip](https://www.yatra.com/flight-schedule/mumbai-to-delhi-flights.html) around this window are advised to book flights early to secure better fares.

### **How do you travel to and from Delhi airport by metro, taxi or train?**

Delhi is served by **Indira Gandhi International Airport (DEL)**, India's busiest airport by passenger volume. IGI connects Delhi to major domestic cities including Mumbai, Bengaluru, Kolkata, Hyderabad, and Chennai, alongside international routes across Asia, Europe, the Middle East, and North America. The airport operates three passenger terminals — T1 for domestic low-cost carriers, T2 for select domestic operations, and T3 for international and full-service [domestic flights](https://www.yatra.com/domestic-flights/) — located roughly 16–20 km from central Delhi. The Delhi Metro Airport Express Line connects T3 to New Delhi Railway Station in about 20 minutes, alongside prepaid taxis, app-based cabs, and airport coach services for travellers heading into the city. Delhi is equally well connected by rail, with New Delhi Railway Station, Hazrat Nizamuddin, and Anand Vihar Terminal linking the capital to destinations across the country, making it a practical hub for combining flights with onward train journeys.

### **What is the best time to visit Delhi for sightseeing and pleasant weather?**

October to March is the most comfortable period to visit Delhi, with cooler temperatures suited to sightseeing and a calendar packed with winter festivals and cultural events. Summers (April–June) bring intense heat that can make outdoor sightseeing difficult by midday, while the monsoon (July–September) brings humidity and intermittent rain that can disrupt travel plans. Booking Delhi flights during the October–March window typically means better weather for exploring landmarks like the Red Fort and India Gate on foot, and is generally considered peak season for both leisure and cultural travel to the capital.

**About Mumbai**

Mumbai, India's financial capital, is a city of contrasts — colonial-era architecture standing beside glass-fronted towers, fishing villages from before the city existed sitting minutes from Bollywood studios. Travellers booking Delhi to Mumbai flights on Yatra.com land in a city that works equally well as a business stopover and a leisure base, with easy access to colonial landmarks, coastal stretches, and one of India's most energetic entertainment and nightlife scenes. As the country's commercial and entertainment hub, Mumbai draws a mix of business travellers, film industry visitors, and tourists exploring its blend of history and modern energy.

### **What are the best beaches near Mumbai for a quick getaway?**

Mumbai's coastline offers several beaches within easy reach of the city, though most are better suited to a stroll, sunset, or street snack than a swim. Juhu Beach, close to the airport, is the city's most popular stretch, known for its evening crowds, kite-flying, and beachside food stalls selling bhel puri and pav bhaji. Versova and Aksa beaches, both in the northern suburbs, are quieter alternatives favoured by joggers and early-morning walkers. For a longer day trip, Alibaug, reached by ferry from the Gateway of India or by road via the Mumbai Trans Harbour Link, offers cleaner sands, old forts, and a more relaxed pace than the city beaches. Travellers with a free afternoon after landing in Mumbai often head straight to Juhu before exploring the rest of the city.

### **Where can you experience Mumbai's Bollywood and film city connection?**

Mumbai is the heart of the Hindi film industry, often referred to as Bollywood, and the city's relationship with cinema is visible everywhere from Bandra's celebrity bungalows to the film studios scattered across the suburbs. Film City in Goregaon offers guided tours of working studio sets, giving visitors a look at how Bollywood productions are filmed. Bandra's Carter Road and Pali Hill are known for celebrity sightings and a concentration of film industry offices, cafés, and production houses. The Gateway of India and Marine Drive frequently double as filming locations and remain popular for visitors retracing scenes from familiar films. For travellers interested in the industry beyond the surface, several tour operators run dedicated Bollywood-themed city tours covering studios, iconic filming spots, and the neighbourhoods where the industry is based.

### **What is Mumbai's nightlife and entertainment scene like?**

Mumbai has one of India's most active nightlife scenes, concentrated in neighbourhoods like Bandra, Lower Parel, and Colaba. Rooftop bars and lounges across the city offer skyline views paired with live music and DJ sets, while Bandra's Linking Road and Hill Road area is dense with bars, pubs, and late-night cafés that stay busy well past midnight. Lower Parel's converted mill compounds host some of the city's larger nightclubs and live performance venues. For a quieter evening, South Mumbai's heritage bars near Colaba Causeway offer a more old-world drinking experience, often housed in buildings dating back to the colonial era. Most nightlife venues in Mumbai operate until around 1:30 AM, in line with state licensing regulations, so travellers planning a night out should check closing times in advance.

### **When is Ganesh Chaturthi celebrated in Mumbai and how does it affect travel?**

Mumbai's biggest annual festival, Ganesh Chaturthi, typically falls between August and September and transforms the city for about ten days. Elaborately decorated idols of Lord Ganesha are installed in homes and public pandals across the city, with the festival culminating in large processions that carry idols to the Arabian Sea for immersion. Areas like Lalbaugcha Raja draw some of the largest crowds in the city during this period. Travellers flying in from Delhi during Ganesh Chaturthi should expect heavier traffic near immersion routes and should plan extra time for airport transfers, particularly on the final two days of the festival.

### **How do you travel from Mumbai airport into the city by metro, taxi or train?**

Mumbai is served by Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj International Airport (BOM), India's second-busiest airport by passenger volume and the arrival point for most Delhi to Mumbai flights. The airport operates two terminals — T1, used primarily by domestic low-cost carriers, and T2, which handles [international flights](https://www.yatra.com/international-flights/) along with domestic flights from full-service carriers. The two terminals are not connected airside and sit a few kilometres apart, with a free shuttle bus running between them; travellers connecting between terminals should allow extra time. The airport is located roughly 25–30 km from South Mumbai, and is accessible on arrival via Mumbai Metro Line 3, prepaid taxis, app-based cabs, and BEST bus services.
